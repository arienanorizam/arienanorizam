SFM: Food Security & Bioscience Math

Oil Palm FFB Yield Optimization

Utilizing calculus to analyze Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) accumulation rates for precision agricultural interventions in Malaysia.

The Bioscience Problem

To guarantee national and global food security, Malaysian plantation managers must optimize the yield of oil palm trees (Elaeis guineensis).

The cumulative weight gain of Fresh Fruit Bunches (FFB) per tree follows a **Logistic Growth Curve**. By calculating the **first derivative $f'(x)$**, we discover the exact day when the growth rate peaks (the inflection point). This dictates the optimal window for targeted fertilizer application to maximize sustainable output.

Mathematical Modeling

Yield Function $f(x)$:
$$f(x) = \frac{L}{1 + e^{-k(x - x_0)}}$$
Growth Rate Derivative $f'(x)$:
$$f'(x) = k \cdot f(x) \left(1 - \frac{f(x)}{L}\right)$$

Where constants are calibrated to MPOB field standards:

• $L = 25\text{ kg}$ (Maximum potential FFB capacity per bunch)

• $k = 0.08$ (Growth steepness coefficient)

• $x_0 = 90\text{ days}$ (Midpoint of typical 180-day bunch maturation cycle)

Data Context & Reference: Growth dynamics modeled after physiological yield frameworks published in the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) Journal of Oil Palm Research.
